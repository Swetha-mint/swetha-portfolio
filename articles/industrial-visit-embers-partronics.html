From Classrooms to Industry: Learning Through Our Visit to Embers and Partronics

22 August 2026 · Pimpri-Chinchwad, Maharashtra

On 22 August 2026, our class went on an industrial visit to Embers and Partronics, giving us an opportunity to step outside the classroom and observe how engineering concepts are applied in real industrial environments. The visit helped us connect academic learning with practical systems involving electronics, automation, communication, manufacturing and product development.

At Embers, we encountered concepts related to automated systems, sensors, RF signals, GPS and geo-tagging, communication gateways, protocols, machine monitoring and system integration. These observations showed us that modern industrial systems depend on the interaction between hardware, software, communication and control.

We also explored ideas around CNC and machining, machine operation, tolerances, measurements and the practical considerations involved in engineering and manufacturing. Discussions around machine health and monitoring raised questions about how sensors and data can be used to understand equipment condition and improve maintenance.

The visit also highlighted the importance of requirements and problem definition. Engineering does not begin simply by building a product. Engineers need to investigate the problem, understand requirements, identify constraints and determine what a user or industry actually needs.

Our visit to Partronics further connected these ideas with electronics manufacturing. Together, the visits gave us a broader view of how design, electronics, manufacturing, integration and communication contribute to real-world products and systems.

As a Semiconductor Engineering student, the experience was particularly meaningful because it showed me that a semiconductor device is one part of a much larger technological ecosystem. Chips, sensors, controllers, communication systems, software and machines have to work together as a complete system.

The visit also left me with questions that can become future project directions: How can machines be monitored using sensors? How can industrial data be communicated efficiently? How can hardware and software be integrated into a single system? How can machine health be tracked? And how can engineers identify the right problem before developing a solution?

The experience reminded us that engineering is not just about understanding individual technologies; it is about understanding how those technologies come together to solve real problems.
